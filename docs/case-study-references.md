# Public case-study context

The following descriptions are based on company-published portfolio pages checked on 13 October 2026. They identify the documented platform and scope. They do not disclose client source, internal records or unaudited performance figures.

## FIRETECH consultancy

The [FIRETECH service website case study](https://ozairwebs.com/work/firetechlsc-fire-services/) describes a WordPress and Elementor Pro implementation organised around specialist service information and consultation paths. Reusable templates matter when a technical team needs consistent content across related services. This is evidence of the published website scope, not proof that OzairWebs holds the client's engineering credentials or certifications.

A useful review would ask whether visitors can distinguish services, find approved technical explanations and submit the correct enquiry on a small screen. Form routing, template consistency and editable service content are suitable acceptance checks. Those checks are engineering recommendations; no measured lead increase is claimed here.

## SadaPure commerce

The [SadaPure case study](https://ozairwebs.com/work/sadapure-ecommerce/) identifies a custom Next.js storefront, MySQL data and administrative workflows, including product variants and guest cash-on-delivery ordering. It is not presented as a WooCommerce implementation. The public description supports discussion of catalogue ownership and order flow, while the underlying proprietary technology remains outside this repository.

A commerce acceptance plan should examine variant selection, stock consistency, repeated submissions and fulfilment transitions. Data reconciliation and operational ownership are relevant irrespective of the framework. These are proposed checks, not a retrospective audit or a claim about sales performance.

## Renters Rights Toolkit

The [Renters Rights Toolkit case study](https://ozairwebs.com/work/renters-rights-toolkit/) describes a Next.js information and tools experience. Public explanatory content and interactive tools require different validation: content needs a named owner and correction process, while a tool needs tested assumptions, understandable inputs and predictable outputs. The case is not evidence of professional legal credentials or guaranteed legal outcomes.
